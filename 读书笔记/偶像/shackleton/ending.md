**Two details that don't add up in the final scene of Lansing's Endurance**

Dear Mr. Philbrick,

I've read Lansing's *Endurance* perhaps ten times. I'm writing because two details in the closing section, right before the epilogue, have puzzled me for years. Your introduction sent me back to this book once again, and I can't think of anyone better placed to help me think them through.

First, the weeping. Lansing ends the crossing with: "Some said that Sørlle turned away and wept." I find this scene does not make sense at all. Why did Sørlle weep instead of Shackleton? A. They were not close, and those Norwegian whalers were tough guys. B. If there was anyone who would cry it should be Shackleton, not Sørlle. So I compared this with the accounts of the two men who were actually present (Tom Crean left no account at all), and neither records anyone weeping: Shackleton (in *South*) and Worsley (in *Shackleton's Boat Journey*) both describe the meeting without any tears — and their versions even conflict about what Sørlle first said. In the version Caroline Alexander quotes, it is the whaler himself who weeps ("Me — I turn away and weep"), not Sørlle.

Second, Anton Andersen. Lansing writes that the men first asked to be taken to Anton Andersen, and were told by the foreman that he had been replaced by the new manager, Sørlle. But neither Shackleton nor Worsley mentions this exchange — both simply say they were directed to, or asked for, Sørlle. It seems unlikely both eyewitnesses would independently omit the same episode, which makes me wonder where Lansing's version came from.

As someone who has faced the same challenge of reconstructing a narrative from thin and sometimes conflicting survivor testimony, how do you read moments like these?
